\title{Analytic Endpoints, Causal Obstructions, and Adaptive Certificates\\
\large A structural program for uniform equilibrium in finite stochastic games}
\author{Working manuscript}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The existence of a uniform equilibrium payoff in every finite stochastic game
remains open for three or more players.  This paper records a body of new
structural results developed around that problem and places them against the
frontier known through 2025.  The program factors the conjecture into three
layers.  First, every finite game admits a coupled real-analytic germ of
stationary discounted equilibria.  Second, the germ has a finite endpoint
classification expressed in terms of harmonic directions, support faces,
owned occupation measures, and zero-pairing obstructions.  Third, an adaptive
certificate, once constructed, implies a uniform equilibrium by a short
quantitative verification theorem.

The first and third layers, and the endpoint classification itself, have been
formalized in Lean.  A classical example of Sorin gives an essential correction
to the intended bridge: even a rational analytic discounted Nash germ may have
an endpoint outside the entire uniform-equilibrium payoff set.  Thus the germ
is source data, not a target which can always be preserved.  Since the germ
itself is existential, the missing theorem may choose a favorable analytic
branch; it must then select an implementable payoff target and construct an
actual public response which is credible against unilateral deviations,
transports that entire selected target, enters legally after public histories,
and either decreases a well-founded rank or discharges a realized account.  We prove exact auxiliary
theorems for direct closure by unilateral occupation harmlessness, analytic
Markov occupation at multiple scales, finite-tree and stopped-child
composition, causal versus ordinary transport, public separator compression,
colored circulation, entry, monitoring, and finite-memory obstructions.  We
also give small counterexamples to several tempting closure principles.

The paper is deliberately status-aware.  Results are labelled as
machine-checked, proved mathematically but not yet formalized, conditional, or
open.  No claim of priority is made for a statement merely because its exact
form appears to be new in this program.  The final section isolates the
remaining conjecture-level arrow and a finite collection of scoped mathematical
problems whose joint solution would close it.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and the frontier}

Let $I$ be a finite set of players, $S$ a finite state space, and
$A_i(s)$ a nonempty finite action set.  A finite stochastic game consists of
bounded stage payoffs $g_i(s,a)$ and a transition kernel $q(\cdot\mid s,a)$.
A behavior profile $\sigma$ is a \emph{uniform $\varepsilon$-equilibrium from
$s$ with payoff $v$} if, for all sufficiently large horizons $N$, its expected
Ces\`aro payoff is within $\varepsilon$ of $v$ and every unilateral behavior
deviation gains at most $\varepsilon$.  The same profile must work for all
large $N$; a family chosen separately for each horizon is not enough.

Throughout, the current state and past action profiles are publicly observed,
players have perfect recall, and behavior strategies use independent private
randomization.  No exogenous public correlation device is part of the base
model.  Whenever a construction calls for a public lottery, it must synthesize
that lottery from publicly observed play and prove unilateral robustness,
entry safety, and sublinear cost.  Granting a free public seed would change the
problem being solved.

Shapley's discounted fixed-point theorem gives stationary equilibria for every
discount factor in the two-player zero-sum model \cite{Shapley1953}; Fink's
theorem gives stationary discounted equilibria in finite $n$-player games
\cite{Fink1964}.  In the zero-sum case, Bewley and Kohlberg obtained a
Puiseux expansion and the asymptotic value \cite{BewleyKohlberg1976}, while
Mertens and Neyman constructed strategies realizing the uniform value
\cite{MertensNeyman1981}.  The multiperson existence problem is qualitatively
different: discounted equilibria may select strategically incompatible
limits, and the punishment needed after a deviation may itself require a
long-run construction.

The principal general positive results before the present program include
Vieille's two-player theorem \cite{Vieille2000I,Vieille2000II}, Solan's
three-player absorbing theorem \cite{Solan1999}, the theory of quitting games
\cite{SolanVieille2001}, and autonomous correlated equilibrium for arbitrary
finite games \cite{SolanVieille2002}.  The Big Match already shows why
calendar-time stationary or bounded-memory reasoning is insufficient:
Blackwell and Ferguson's uniform strategy is genuinely history adaptive
\cite{BlackwellFerguson1968}.  Recent work proves that only logarithmically
many public memory states need be used by carefully scheduled strategies in
certain formulations, while bounded public memory still fails for the Big
Match \cite{HansenIbsenJensenNeyman2025}.  A late-2025 result establishes an
undiscounted equilibrium payoff for positive recursive absorbing games whose
nonabsorbing components are nonrectangular \cite{SolanVieille2025}.  These
advances do not settle arbitrary finite games with three or more players.

The program here starts from a simple diagnosis.  The limiting discounted
payoff is not itself the missing object and, in general, is not even an
admissible uniform target.  Sorin's absorbing example
(\S\ref{sec:sorin-target}) has a rational analytic discounted-equilibrium
payoff whose endpoint is disjoint from the uniform-equilibrium payoff set.
What is missing is therefore a mechanism that uses analytic endpoint data to
select an implementable target and then produces one strategy profile
satisfying, at once,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item delivery of the selected full vector target;
\item a cap against every unilateral history-dependent deviation;
\item credibility of every public response after the history at which it is
      invoked;
\item compatibility with public suffix rebasing and random stopping;
\item strict progress in a well-founded recursion, or a complete quantitative
      account showing why no progress is needed.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Status convention}

Four labels are used throughout.
\begin{itemize}
\item \leanchecked: a theorem has a proof in the accompanying Lean
      development, with no additional axiom.
\item \proved: a complete finite-dimensional or probabilistic proof is given
      here or in the associated question file, but the theorem is not claimed
      to be fully formalized.
\item \conditional: the implication is rigorous, but one or more strategic
      inputs are explicit hypotheses rather than consequences of an arbitrary
      game.
\item \openstatus: the statement is a genuine remaining problem.
\end{itemize}
At the stable audited snapshot the development has one intentional admitted
declaration, exactly at the global uniform-equilibrium constructor.  The
semantic verifier, analytic-germ existence, root endpoint classification, and
several special cases are checked.  None of the thirteen generic nonsemantic
endpoint leaves has yet been closed unconditionally.

\section{The certificate that would settle the game}

The most useful contribution of formalization has been to force the final
verification statement into a form that does not conceal a strategy in a
limit or a quantifier swap.

Fix an initial state $s_0$ and target $v\in\RR^I$.  The semantic waist is a
uniform deviation-cap constructor: for each error it returns one behavior
profile and one horizon threshold such that all longer finite averages deliver
$v$ and every unilateral behavior deviation is capped by $v$ plus the error.
This is equivalent to $v$ being a uniform-equilibrium payoff.

One sound sufficient language is the checked adaptive-potential certificate.
At error $\delta$ it consists, schematically, of a public behavior profile
$\sigma$, three public-history potentials for each player (on-path lower,
on-path upper, and deviation upper), and Cesaro-small error budgets.  The
initial expected potentials are close to $v$.  Their expected values from
$s_0$ are respectively submartingale or supermartingale sequences, and bound
the corresponding expected stage payoff in the required direction.  The
deviation potential inequality is quantified over every unilateral behavior
strategy.  The potentials need not be bounded or close to $v$ after the
initial history.

This expectation-level formulation is load-bearing: it permits cancellation
between publicly observed stochastic branches.  More local bounded-potential,
account, and stopped-child systems are useful construction languages only
when a theorem compiles them into this certificate or directly into the
semantic deviation caps.  The proof does not require stationary play or a
single scalar Lyapunov function.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive-certificate verifier; \leanchecked]
\label{thm:verifier}
Suppose that for every $\delta>0$ a finite stochastic game admits an adaptive
potential certificate at $(s_0,v,\delta)$.  Then $v$ is a uniform equilibrium
payoff from $s_0$.  Quantitatively, the strategy contained in the
$\delta$-certificate has, for all sufficiently large $N$,
\[
 \left\|\EE_\sigma\frac1N\sum_{t<N}g(t)-v\right\|_\infty
 \le C\delta+o_N(1)
\]
and, for every $i$ and every unilateral behavior deviation $\alpha_i$,
\[
 \EE_{\alpha_i,\sigma_{-i}}\frac1N\sum_{t<N}g_i(t)
 \le v_i+C\delta+o_N(1),
\]
where the remainder is uniform over the deviation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof mechanism]
Apply the lower and upper expectation-level telescope to the prescribed
profile, and the upper telescope to every deviating profile.  Monotonicity of
the expected potentials removes the terminal term in the favorable direction;
their initial values anchor the estimate at $v$.  The Cesaro-small budgets
contribute $C\delta$.  Construction-specific bounded potentials, accounts,
and stopped systems have their own compilation theorems before this verifier
is invoked.  The prescribed lower and upper directions give delivery.
The quantifiers are
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\sigma\ \exists N_0\
 \forall N\ge N_0\ \forall i\ \forall\alpha_i,
\]
so the deviation may depend on $N$ and on the entire observed history.
This is the argument formalized by the certificate verifier.
\end{proof}

The theorem is substantive, but it is not the conjecture in disguise only if
the certificate fields are concrete: action kernels, transition laws,
targets, bounded potentials, and proved ledgers.  A record whose decisive
field simply returns a uniform equilibrium or an adaptive certificate is only
an interface.  This distinction will recur below.

\section{Analytic Bellman germs and the endpoint atlas}

For normalized discount $\lambda\in(0,1)$, let
\[
 V_i^\lambda(s)=
 \EE_{s,\sigma^\lambda}
 \sum_{t\ge0}\lambda(1-\lambda)^t g_i(s_t,a_t),
\]
where $\sigma^\lambda$ is a stationary equilibrium.  The Bellman equilibrium
conditions are a finite system of polynomial equalities and inequalities in
$\lambda$, the mixed actions, and $V$.  Compactness supplies accumulation,
but an arbitrary sequence loses sign and order information.  Semialgebraic
curve selection retains it.

\begin{theorem}[Coupled analytic Bellman germ; \leanchecked]
\label{thm:germ}
Every finite stochastic game admits, after restricting to a sufficiently
small interval and using a finite analytic reparameterization when necessary,
a real-analytic arc
\[
 t\longmapsto
 \bigl(\lambda(t),\sigma(t),V(t)\bigr),\qquad t\downarrow0,
\]
of stationary discounted equilibria, with $\lambda(t)>0$ and
$\lambda(t)\to0$.  Every equilibrium support, every equality pattern in the
Bellman inequalities, and the sign of every nonzero analytic comparison is
constant on the punctured arc.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Construction]
Encode normalization, simplex constraints, Bellman equalities on used
actions, and Bellman inequalities on unused actions as a compact
semialgebraic set whose projection accumulates at $\lambda=0$.  Real
semialgebraic curve selection gives a semialgebraic arc.  One-dimensional
semialgebraic preparation yields a convergent Puiseux parameterization; a
power substitution converts it to a real-analytic germ.  Finiteness of the
comparison family and the identity theorem allow a common punctured interval
on which every support and sign cell is fixed.
\end{proof}

Write $v=V(0)$.  Expanding the Bellman identities produces successive bias
vectors and occupation constraints.  A direction already accounted for is
placed in a processed harmonic span.  The first response outside that span
falls into a finite alternative governed by:
\begin{itemize}
\item semantic closure;
\item a proper support face;
\item a player-owned positive occupation or circulation;
\item a full-support zero-pairing configuration;
\item failure of target transport, entry, ownership, or credibility;
\item a new harmonic direction.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Finite endpoint classification; \leanchecked]
\label{thm:atlas}
For every analytic Bellman germ, processed harmonic span, and legal entry
datum, a first nonprocessed hierarchy response exists and belongs to one of a
finite list of typed endpoint leaves.  The classification is exhaustive and
retains the player, target, support, analytic order, and occupation data needed
by the corresponding leaf.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is an obstruction atlas, not a closure theorem.  One leaf is
semantic.  The other thirteen accept reconstruction data; at present no
generic theorem manufactures that data from an arbitrary game.  Thus the
strongest unconditional chain is
\[
 \text{finite game}
 \longrightarrow \text{analytic Bellman germ}
 \longrightarrow \text{honest endpoint leaf}
 \not\!\longrightarrow \text{adaptive certificate}.
\]
This explicit broken arrow is the current frontier.

The quantifier over arcs matters.  The atlas classifies every chosen analytic
germ, but the conjecture requires only the existence of one successful
construction.  A global proof may therefore select or replace an equilibrium
arc so as to avoid an unclosable leaf; it need not close every germ
individually.  This ``arc improvement'' route is logically distinct from leaf
closure.  Sorin's example in \S\ref{sec:sorin-target} also shows its limit:
even choosing a better discounted arc cannot suffice when every discounted
equilibrium payoff has the wrong target.  Arc selection and endogenous target
selection are separate gates.

\section{Local analytic consequences}

Several useful conclusions do follow from the germ without solving the
strategic routing problem.

\subsection{Invisible neutral actions}

Let $p(t),r(t)\in\Delta(S)$, $W(t)=W_0+t^qH(t)$, and suppose
\[
 t^qg(t)+(1-t^q)(r(t)-p(t))\cdot W(t)\le0,
 \qquad
 (r(0)-p(0))\cdot W_0=0.
\]
Put $D(t)=(r(t)-p(t))\cdot W_0$.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp neutral-drift dichotomy; \proved]
\label{prop:neutral-drift}
If $D\not\equiv0$ and $m=\ord_0D<q$, then the leading coefficient of
$D$ is negative and $-D(t)\ge ct^m$ for all small positive $t$.  Otherwise
$D=t^qL$ for an analytic germ $L$, and
\[
 g(0)+(r(0)-p(0))\cdot H(0)+L(0)\le0.
\]
If $W_0$ is nonconstant, the first branch yields a bounded centered public
state score with expected deviation drift at least $c't^m$.  If $W_0$ is
constant, then $D\equiv0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $A=r-p$ reduces the inequality to
\[
 (1-t^q)D(t)+t^qB(t)\le0,
 \qquad
 B(t)=g(t)+(1-t^q)A(t)\cdot H(t).
\]
If $D=d_mt^m+O(t^{m+1})$ with $m<q$, division by $t^m$ and passage to the
limit gives $d_m\le0$, hence $d_m<0$.  The quantitative bound follows by
tail control.  In the other branch analytic divisibility gives $D=t^qL$;
divide by $t^q$ and pass to the limit.  Centering
$W_0$ does not change $A\cdot W_0$ because $A\cdot\one=0$, and normalization
gives the bounded monitor.
\end{proof}

For finitely many neutral unused actions, simultaneous representation of all
$L_a(0)$ by one state potential is a finite linear feasibility problem.  Its
failure has an exact Farkas certificate: a signed combination of action
transition rows which annihilates every state potential but pairs nontrivially
with the vector of coefficients.  Importantly, the certificate need not be
owned by one player; mixed-player obstructions are real.

\subsection{One-dimensional moving-bias minimax}

For a finite analytic family of one-step zero-sum linear programs, the active
basis and sign pattern stabilize after shrinking the parameter interval.
Consequently an optimal selector can be chosen analytic and with support at
most two in the one-dimensional moving-bias problem.  The exact remainder is
the first nonvanishing determinant (equivalently, reduced-cost) jet of the
stabilized basis.  This gives a useful local minimax selector and makes all
cancellation orders finite and computable.  It does not by itself supply
target delivery or a credible multiplayer punishment.

\subsection{Saturated Puiseux rank}

For a finite family of convergent Laurent--Puiseux series, saturation by the
common value group produces a finite lexicographic obstruction rank: leading
valuation, active support dimension, and the first nonzero oriented
coefficient.  Rank comparison stabilizes effectively from coefficient-tail
bounds.  Saturation alone forgets positivity, however; strategic descent
requires a nonnegative occupation cone, not merely the linear span of leading
jets.

\section{Multiscale occupation of analytic Markov kernels}

The following theorem isolates the probabilistic content of a moving analytic
kernel.  It is useful both for block constructions and for diagnosing what
the germ does \emph{not} imply.

Let $K(t)$ be an $N\times N$ stochastic matrix analytic at $0$.  After
shrinking the interval, its punctured support graph is fixed.  Let
$C_1,\dots,C_m$ be its closed strongly connected components, let $\pi_j(t)$
be the stationary law on $C_j$, and let $\alpha_j(\mu,t)$ be the probability
of eventual absorption in $C_j$ from initial law $\mu$.

\begin{theorem}[Analytic Markov occupation decomposition; \proved]
\label{thm:analytic-markov}
The functions $\pi_j(t)$ and $\alpha_j(\mu,t)$ extend analytically to
$t=0$.  If $\Pi(t)$ is the Ces\`aro projection and
\[
 G(t)=(I-K(t)+\Pi(t))^{-1}(I-\Pi(t)),
\]
then $G$ is meromorphic with a pole of finite order $p$.  For every bounded
$f:S\to\RR$, every initial law $\mu$, and every integer $L\ge1$,
\[
 \sum_{n<L}\mu K(t)^nf
 =L\sum_{j=1}^m\alpha_j(\mu,t)\,\pi_j(t)f+R_L(t),
 \qquad |R_L(t)|\le 2\|f\|_\infty C t^{-p}.
\]
If every positive transition has vanishing order at most $q$, one may take
the safe bound $p\le Nq$; a sharper bound is obtained from the maximal order
of a directed forest used in the relevant matrix-tree formulas.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Markov-chain tree theorem writes every recurrent stationary coordinate as
a quotient of finite sums of positive analytic monomials.  Extracting the
minimal common order removes the apparent pole; boundedness makes it
removable.  The same argument, or Cramer's rule for the transient absorption
system, treats $\alpha_j$.  The inverse defining $G$ is meromorphic by the
adjugate formula.  Finally,
\[
 \sum_{n<L}K^nf=L\Pi f+(I-K^L)Gf,
\]
because $(I-K)G=I-\Pi$ and $K\Pi=\Pi$.  Uniformity in $\mu$ and $L$ follows
from stochastic contraction and the pole bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The estimate separates the mixing debt $O(t^{-p})$ from the stationary
mean.  A block of length $L\gg t^{-p}$ samples the latter.  It does not say
that a single calendar realizes the discounted endpoint: when transitions
between recurrent classes have probability of order $\lambda$, those fluxes
are visible on the discounted time scale $1/\lambda$ even though they vanish
in the endpoint kernel.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Abel endpoint and the limits of one global calendar}

The global-calendar question has a negative answer.  Its resolution also
gives a useful exact formula for the singular Abel endpoint.

This subsection assumes that the transition family is analytic in the
normalized discount $\lambda$ itself.  The general germ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:germ} is only analytic in a parameter $t$ and may satisfy
$\lambda(t)\asymp t^d$.  A transition of order $t^a$ is then faster than,
critical with, or slower than the discount scale according as
$a<d$, $a=d$, or $a>d$.  Formula (5.1) is therefore the one-scale
specialization of the coupled-scale reduction stated below.  Faster classes
must first be quotiented, a generator is retained when its scale matches
killing, and slower irreversible leakage is discarded by the Abel resolvent
but retained as a separate feedback hazard.

Put $P_0=P(0)$.  Let $C_1,\dots,C_m$ be its recurrent classes.  Write
$H_{xi}$ for the probability, under $P_0$, of eventual absorption in $C_i$,
and let row $i$ of $J$ be the stationary law of $P_0$ on $C_i$.  Then
\[
 P_0H=H,\qquad JP_0=J,\qquad JH=I_m,\qquad \Pi_0=HJ.
\]
Set
\[
 P_1=P'(0),\qquad A=JP_1H,\qquad g=Jr(0).
\]
The matrix $A$ is a continuous-time Markov generator: its off-diagonal
entries are nonnegative and $A\one=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Reduced Abel endpoint and schedule reduction; \proved]
\label{thm:abel-schedule}
For every finite analytic family $P(\lambda),r(\lambda)$,
\[
 \lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}
 \lambda\bigl(I-(1-\lambda)P(\lambda)\bigr)^{-1}r(\lambda)
 =H(I_m-A)^{-1}g.                                \tag{5.1}
\]
Thus existence of the Abel endpoint is automatic.

Let $\alpha_t>0$ be any nonincreasing parameter sequence tending to zero,
including the flattening of an arbitrary positive-parameter block schedule,
and put
\[
 Q_0=I,\qquad Q_{t+1}=Q_tP(\alpha_t),\qquad
 K_N=\frac1N\sum_{t<N}Q_tH.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{t<N}Q_tr(\alpha_t)-K_Ng\longrightarrow0.      \tag{5.2}
\]
Consequently the schedule realizes the Abel endpoint at every horizon and
from every initial state if and only if
\[
 \bigl(K_N-H(I_m-A)^{-1}\bigr)g\longrightarrow0.             \tag{5.3}
\]
Restarting the block clock adds no condition: row-uniform convergence in
(5.3) supplies the same modulus from every restarted state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The discounted resolvent identity is
\[
 [I-(1-\lambda)P(\lambda)]V(\lambda)=\lambda r(\lambda).
\]
Every subsequential limit $w$ is $P_0$-harmonic, so $w=Hu$.  Multiplication
by $J/\lambda$ and passage to the limit gives
$(I_m-A)u=g$.  Since $A$ is a Markov generator, $I_m-A$ is invertible, proving
(5.1).

For (5.2), take the unique $\psi$ satisfying
\[
 (I-P_0)\psi=r(0)-Hg,\qquad J\psi=0.
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
 Q_t(r(0)-Hg)
 &=(Q_t-Q_{t+1})\psi
   +Q_t(P(\alpha_t)-P_0)\psi .
\end{align*}
The first term telescopes.  The Ces\`aro means of the second term and of
$Q_t(r(\alpha_t)-r(0))$ vanish because $\alpha_t\to0$.  This proves (5.2),
and (5.3) follows from (5.1).
\end{proof}

Condition (5.3) is an exact finite-dimensional \emph{trajectory reduction},
not a static decidability criterion: it still asks whether one ordered
nonhomogeneous Markov trajectory reaches a prescribed Ces\`aro class kernel.

\subsection{Canonical coupled-scale reduction and feedback boundary}

Put
\[
 Q(t)=P(t)-I,
 \qquad
 \gamma(t)=\frac{\lambda(t)}{1-\lambda(t)}
            =ct^d+o(t^d),\qquad c>0.
\]
Then the normalized discounted kernel is
\[
 \gamma(t)\bigl(\gamma(t)I-Q(t)\bigr)^{-1}.
\]
For a finite generator $G$, let $H_G$ be its absorption map into closed
recurrent classes, $J_G$ the matrix of their stationary laws, and
$\Pi_G=H_GJ_G$.  If an analytic generator germ $A(t)$ satisfies $A(0)=G$,
there is a uniquely normalized analytic invariant graph
\[
 U(t)=H_G+W(t),\qquad J_GW(t)=0,\qquad
 A(t)U(t)=U(t)B(t),\qquad B(t)=J_GA(t)U(t).
\]
This removes transient directions and records mediated excursions.  For
example, if $A(t)=G+tA_1+t^2A_2+\cdots$, then
\[
 B_1=J_GA_1H_G,\qquad
 B_2=J_GA_2H_G-J_GA_1G^\#A_1H_G.
\]

Iterate this reduction.  At a level whose physical generator is
$t^\alpha A(t)$, quotient the recurrent classes of $A(0)$.  If the first
nonzero reduced term has order $\beta<d-\alpha$, it is faster than killing
and becomes the next generator at physical order $\alpha+\beta$.  If
$\beta=d-\alpha$, retain its leading generator $\Gamma$ without a further
quotient.  If no term occurs through that order, set $\Gamma=0$.  Let
$\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal J$ be the products of the absorption and stationary
maps over the faster levels.

\begin{theorem}[Coupled-scale Abel reduction; \proved]
\label{thm:multiscale-abel}
The iteration terminates after at most $|S|-1$ nontrivial faster-than-killing
quotients, followed by at most one critical solve, and
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}
 \lambda(t)\bigl(I-(1-\lambda(t))P(t)\bigr)^{-1}r(t)
 =\mathcal H\,c(cI-\Gamma)^{-1}\mathcal J r(0).       \tag{5.5}
\]
The hierarchy and the physical limit are invariant, up to relabeling, under
analytic reparameterization $t=au^q+O(u^{q+1})$.  For effectively represented
rational or real-algebraic germs, all coefficients needed through the killing
order are obtained by finite exact linear algebra.
\end{theorem}

The same analysis separates Abel computation from feedback attainability.
Let $A=[0,t_0)$ be the available parameter rows.  For an initial state $x$ and
target $z$, consider finite nonnegative measures $\mu_s,\eta_s$ on $A$
satisfying
\begin{align*}
 \mu_y(A)&=\sum_s\int_A P(t)(s,y)\,d\mu_s(t),\\
 \mathbf 1_{\{y=x\}}+\sum_s\int_A P(t)(s,y)\,d\eta_s(t)
   &=\eta_y(A)+\mu_y(A),\\
 \sum_s\int_A r(t)(s)\,d\mu_s(t)&=z.                 \tag{5.6}
\end{align*}
The first line is recurrent balance; the second is transient entrance flow.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-mode feedback realization; \proved]
\label{thm:feedback-flux}
A fresh-public-randomized finite-mode feedback policy realizes $z$ from $x$
in expected Ces\`aro average if and only if (5.6) is feasible.  Two modes---a
transient entrance mode and a recurrent mode---suffice.  A certificate may be
chosen atomic with support bounded linearly in $|S|$ and the reward dimension.

For any specified deterministic or randomized finite-mode architecture, exact
realization is equivalently tested by the absorption/stationary projection of
its finite augmented Markov chain.  Unrestricted deterministic feedback is
not captured by the closure of (5.6): its entrance problem is nonconvex and
can be expressed by an inverse-limit viability recursion over actual rows.
\end{theorem}

The fresh public randomization in this theorem is a resource of the controlled
Markov formulation, not a free device in the base stochastic game.  A game
theoretic application must synthesize and protect it or use the deterministic
viability route.

\begin{example}[Zero-defect irreversible leak]
\label{ex:abel-leak}
Let
\[
 P(\lambda)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1-\lambda^2&\lambda^2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad r=(0,1)^\mathsf T.
\]
The Abel endpoint is $v=(0,1)^\mathsf T=\Pi_0r$, so its defect is zero.
Nevertheless every schedule with $\alpha_0>0$ creates a permanent positive
probability of absorption in the rewarding state.  Starting from the first
state, if
\[
 q_t=\prod_{s<t}(1-\alpha_s^2),
\]
then the expected Ces\`aro reward converges to $1-q_\infty>0$, rather than
$v_1=0$.  No positive-parameter schedule realizes the endpoint.  If the
architecture permits permanent entry into the exact endpoint mode
$\lambda=0$, this particular obstruction disappears.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Slower leakage and nonclosed exact attainment]
\label{ex:multiscale-nonclosed}
Let $S=\{0,1,2\}$, $\lambda(t)=t$, $r(s)=e_s\in\RR^3$, and
\[
 P(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1-t-t^2&t&t^2\\
 0&1&0\\
 0&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The order-$t$ critical generator sends $0$ to $1$ and discards the slower
order-$t^2$ transition to $2$.  Hence the Abel target from $0$ is
\[
 z=(1/2,1/2,0).
\]
No deterministic or publicly randomized feedback policy realizes $z$
exactly.  Since $2$ is absorbing, the zero third coordinate forces zero
probability of ever entering $2$; therefore every reachable action at state
$0$ must use $t=0$, which makes entry into state $1$ impossible.

Nevertheless $z$ is approximable.  Use $t=\varepsilon$ for $L$ attempts at
state $0$ and then use $t=0$.  With
$q_\varepsilon=1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2$, the limiting occupation is
\[
 \left(q_\varepsilon^L,
 \frac{1-q_\varepsilon^L}{1+\varepsilon},
 \frac{\varepsilon(1-q_\varepsilon^L)}{1+\varepsilon}\right).
\]
Choosing $L=L(\varepsilon)$ so that
$q_\varepsilon^{L(\varepsilon)}\to1/2$ makes these vectors converge to $z$.
Thus exact feedback attainment is nonclosed.  This distinction is crucial for
uniform equilibrium: its profile may depend on the requested accuracy, so an
all-accuracy constructive target correspondence may contain limits which no
single policy realizes exactly.  Closure alone is still insufficient unless
the horizon and unilateral-deviation estimates are carried with the
approximation.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Coordinatewise realization without a common calendar]
\label{ex:abel-vector}
Take two disjoint one-way chains with hazards $\lambda$ and $2\lambda$, and
two reward coordinates recording absorption in the corresponding chains.
The Abel target survival probabilities are $1/2$ and $1/3$.  A common
schedule would require
\[
 \prod_t(1-\alpha_t)=\frac12,\qquad
 \prod_t(1-2\alpha_t)=\frac13.
\]
But for every $0<x<1/2$,
\[
 -\log(1-2x)>2[-\log(1-x)],
\]
which would force $\log3>2\log2=\log4$.  Each coordinate separately is
realizable by assigning the appropriate total log hazard, but no global
scalar calendar realizes both.  State- or context-local clocks evade this
particular incompatibility because the two components are publicly
distinguishable.
\end{example}

For a scalar one-way hazard
$h(\lambda)=c\lambda^k+o(\lambda^k)$, the resulting trichotomy is sharp.
If $k=1$, the Abel target is realized by total log hazard $\log(1+c)$.
If $k>1$, the Abel target remains in the nonabsorbing class, but every positive
first parameter creates an irreversible delivery error.  A nonvanishing
endpoint hazard instead gives absorption with probability one.

The defect itself always has the finite flux representation
\[
 v-\Pi_0r(0)
 =H\bigl[(I-A)^{-1}-I\bigr]g
 =HA(I-A)^{-1}g.                                  \tag{5.4}
\]
Only order-$\lambda$ interclass transitions enter (5.4).  Example
\ref{ex:abel-leak} shows that higher-order leakage can still obstruct a
positive-parameter schedule, while Example~\ref{ex:abel-vector} shows that
the first-order fluxes need not be jointly implementable by one calendar.

The remaining pure Markov problem is therefore narrower: allow a public
finite-state controller to choose the parameter from the current state and
mode, including permanent use of $\lambda=0$, and characterize restartable
realization.  The strategically relevant extension must additionally preserve
one-controller Bellman caps against arbitrary history-dependent deviations.

\section{Occupation harmlessness and direct strategic closure}

One of the most useful new results is a closure route that does not try to
purify a mixed analytic obstruction.  It asks instead whether every unilateral
occupation is harmless.  This is the correct acceptance test for examples in
which a mixed-player circulation represents a joint improvement that no
single player can implement.

Let $X$ be a finite augmented public state space carrying every finite mode of
a fixed public profile $\sigma$.  Against $\sigma_{-i}$, player $i$ controls a
finite Markov system with actions $A_i(x)$, kernel $P_i(\cdot\mid x,a)$, and a
bounded bookkeeping reward $\rho_i(x,a)$.  Let $\mathcal O_i$ be the polytope
of invariant state--action occupations supported on the part of the arena
reachable by some unilateral strategy from the declared entry.

\begin{theorem}[Direct closure by global unilateral harmlessness; \proved]
\label{thm:direct-closure}
Assume, for every $i$,
\[
 \gamma_i:=\max_{\eta\in\mathcal O_i}
       \sum_{x,a}\eta(x,a)\rho_i(x,a)\le0.                 \tag{6.1}
\]
Assume further that a bounded $L_i:X\to\RR$ and a deterministic
$r_i(n)=o(n)$ compile actual target-relative payoffs into bookkeeping rewards:
for every reachable history ending at $x$, every continuation deviation, and
every $n$,
\begin{align*}
 \EE_h\sum_{t<n}(g_i(t)-v_i)
 \le{}&\EE_h\sum_{t<n}\rho_i(X_t,A_t)
       +L_i(x)-\EE_hL_i(X_n)+r_i(n).             \tag{6.2}
\end{align*}
Finally suppose prescribed play delivers $v$,
\[
 \max_i\left|\EE_\sigma\sum_{t<n}(g_i(t)-v_i)\right|=o(n). \tag{6.3}
\]
Then, for each player separately, there is a bounded potential $h_i$ such
that every randomized history-dependent unilateral deviation satisfies
\[
 \EE_h\sum_{t<n}(g_i(t)-v_i)
 \le \osc(L_i+h_i)+r_i(n).                       \tag{6.4}
\]
Thus the one fixed profile $\sigma$ is a uniform equilibrium profile with
payoff $v$.  No potential common to the players is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The dual of the finite global average-reward occupation LP is
\[
 \min_{\theta,h}\ \theta
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 \rho_i(x,a)+\sum_yP_i(y\mid x,a)h(y)-h(x)\le\theta
\]
on every reachable row.  This is not a unichain reduction: the primal ranges
over every normalized invariant occupation in the whole unilateral-reachable
arena, including every recurrent class.  Its single scalar $\theta$ is the
maximum gain over all those classes.  Finite-dimensional LP duality and (6.1)
therefore give a bounded $h_i$ with the right side at most zero even in the
multichain case.  Under an arbitrary behavior deviation, condition
on the complete history.  Its randomized action is a convex combination of
legal rows, hence
\[
 \EE[\rho_i(X_t,A_t)+h_i(X_{t+1})-h_i(X_t)\mid\mathcal F_t]\le0.
\]
Sum and take expectations to bound the bookkeeping reward by
$h_i(x)-\EE h_i(X_n)$.  Insert this in (6.2) and combine the two endpoint
terms to obtain (6.4).  The argument is uniform over all history dependence
and private randomization.  Equation (6.3) supplies the distinct delivery
half of uniform equilibrium.
\end{proof}

Three qualifications are essential.  First, harmlessness on only a selected
analytic support is insufficient: a deviator may reach another recurrent
class.  Second, the occupation LP supplies the potential inequality but not
the payoff compilation (6.2).  Third, no incentive inequality implies
prescribed delivery (6.3).

\subsection{The discounted endpoint need not be a uniform target}
\label{sec:sorin-target}

The endpoint defect is not merely that the limiting stationary profile may
fail to deliver its own limiting discounted payoff.  The payoff itself may be
impossible as a uniform-equilibrium target.  Sorin's two-player example
\cite{Sorin1986} has one nonabsorbing state and the payoff table
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &L&R\\ \hline
T&(0,2)^*&(1,0)^*\\
B&(1,0)&(0,1).
\end{array}
\tag{6.5}
\]
A star means absorption with the displayed payoff forever; after either bottom
entry the state remains nonabsorbing.  Under normalized $\lambda$-discounting,
put
\[
 p_\lambda=\Pr_1(T)=\frac{\lambda}{2+\lambda},
 \qquad
 q_\lambda=\Pr_2(L)=\frac12.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Against $q_\lambda$, player 1 obtains $1/2$ from either row.  For player 2,
the difference between $L$ and $R$ is
\[
 2p_\lambda-(1-p_\lambda)\lambda=0,
\]
and the common continuation payoff is $2/3$.  Hence this is a stationary Nash
equilibrium with payoff
\[
 V^\lambda=\left(\frac12,\frac23\right)
\tag{6.7}
\]
for every $\lambda>0$.  The germ is rational and analytic at $\lambda=0$.

Nevertheless, the uniform-equilibrium payoff set of (6.5) is
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm unif}
 =\left\{\bigl(\alpha,2(1-\alpha)\bigr):
          \frac12\le\alpha\le\frac23\right\}
\tag{6.8}
\]
\cite{Sorin1986}.  Every point in this segment satisfies
$2w_1+w_2=2$, whereas the discounted endpoint satisfies
\[
 2\left(\frac12\right)+\frac23=\frac53.
\tag{6.9}
\]
The separation is therefore from the entire uniform payoff set, not only from
the payoff generated by the endpoint stationary profile.

The same exclusion survives a commonly observed public randomization device.
Let $\theta$ be the first time player 1 uses $T$.  The standard survival
argument forces $\Pr(\theta=\infty)\to0$ along public-randomized uniform
$\eta$-equilibria as $\eta\downarrow0$: conditional on sufficiently late
survival, continued nonabsorption yields total payoff at most approximately
$1$, while the two uniform minmax guarantees sum to $1/2+2/3=7/6$.
Public signals are part of the conditioning history and are available to the
deviator.  On every path with $\theta<\infty$, the Ces\`aro average of
$2g_1+g_2$ converges to $2$.  Thus public correlation cannot produce the
endpoint (6.7).

The analytic mechanism is transparent.  Since
$p_\lambda\sim\lambda/2$, the normalized discounted mass before absorption is
\[
 \frac{\lambda}
 {1-(1-\lambda)(1-p_\lambda)}
 =\frac{2+\lambda}{3}\longrightarrow\frac23.
\tag{6.10}
\]
The expected nonabsorbing payoff at the endpoint mode is
$(1/2,1/2)$ and the expected absorbing payoff conditional on absorption is
$(1/2,1)$.  Consequently the discounted endpoint is the Abel mixture
\[
 \frac23\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)
 +\frac13\left(\frac12,1\right)
 =\left(\frac12,\frac23\right).
\tag{6.11}
\]
A horizon-free uniform equilibrium cannot retain the positive preabsorption
occupation fraction appearing in this mixture.  The scalar gap $1/3$ in
(6.9) cannot be hidden in a sublinear potential or account.

This example separates two obligations which must not be conflated:
\emph{root target selection} may have to replace the analytic endpoint by a
different payoff, while \emph{internal target transport} must preserve the
complete vector once a recursive node has declared its target.  By contrast,
if actions do not affect the transition matrix, the fixed occupation law and
the classical Abel--Ces\`aro theorem do force endpoint delivery.

\section{Circulations, ownership, and separator compression}

\subsection{Public contextual separators}

Let $E$ be the edges of a finite directed public graph and let
$z:E\to\RR$ be a bounded transition score.  The following equivalence is the
finite graph form of ``compressing a contextual monitor to one potential.''

\begin{theorem}[Cycle--potential alternative; \proved]
\label{thm:cycle-potential}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\sum_e m(e)z(e)\le0$ for every nonnegative circulation $m$;
\item the sum of $z$ on every directed cycle is nonpositive;
\item there exists $h$ on vertices such that
      $z(x,y)+h(y)-h(x)\le0$ on every edge.
\end{enumerate}
If the system is rational, feasibility and failure have rational
primal--dual LP certificates of polynomial encoding size.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every nonnegative circulation is a nonnegative combination of directed
cycles, so (ii) implies (i); a cycle incidence vector gives the converse.
The equivalence with (iii) is the difference-constraints theorem, or linear
programming duality.  Telescoping proves directly that (iii) implies (ii).
\end{proof}

The same theorem holds for a legal row correspondence
$\mathcal K(r)\subseteq\Delta(R)$.  A stationary legal edge occupation
$\ell$ satisfies flow balance and the perspective constraint
$\ell(r,\cdot)\in m(r)\mathcal K(r)$.  A public edge occupation $a$ admits a
strict stationary separator precisely when it lies outside this polytope:
there are $z,h$ with
\[
 \sup_{k\in\mathcal K(r)}
 \sum_{r'}k(r')\bigl[z(r,r')+h(r')-h(r)\bigr]\le0,
 \qquad \langle a,z\rangle>0.                   \tag{7.1}
\]
This is ordinary finite-dimensional separation plus the dual of the legal
occupation LP.

\subsection{Colored circulation}

Suppose every charged edge or row is assigned an owner.  Let $C$ be the cone
of nonnegative circulations and $C_i$ the subcone generated by circulations
using only edges owned by player $i$.

\begin{theorem}[Owner-pure decomposition criterion; \proved]
\label{thm:colored-circulation}
Every nonnegative circulation decomposes as a sum of owner-pure circulations,
\[
 C=\sum_i C_i,
\]
if and only if every directed cycle in the positive support graph is
monochromatic.  Equivalently, $C$ is generated by owner-pure extreme rays.
When the condition fails, a mixed-color simple cycle is a finite certificate.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is elementary but strategically decisive.  Playerwise feasibility
does not imply that a mixed-player separator can be assigned to one deviator.
A finite public cover cannot repair this defect unless it changes the graph:
an owner-purifying cover exists exactly when every lifted simple cycle is
monochromatic.  Thus ``choose an owner later'' is not a legitimate general
step.

\subsection{The mixed-owner holonomy example}

Consider two states $x,y$ alternating deterministically.  At $x$, player 1
chooses $0$ or $1$, with payoffs
\[
 g(x,0)=(1,-1),\qquad g(x,1)=(1,1),
\]
and at $y$, player 2 chooses,
\[
 g(y,0)=(-1,1),\qquad g(y,1)=(1,1).
\]
Take action $0$ at both states.  The normalized discounted values are
\[
 V^\lambda(x)=\frac{\lambda}{2-\lambda}(1,-1),\qquad
 V^\lambda(y)=\frac{\lambda}{2-\lambda}(-1,1),
\]
so the endpoint target is $v=0$.  Both unused owner rows are exact Bellman
equalities.  Half of each row forms a positive mixed-owner circulation with
zero first pairing, but scheduling it produces payoff $(1,1)$ rather than
$0$.  Every owner-pure occupation is target-neutral, no owner-pure positive
subray exists, and a common nonnegative bridge would require
\[
 \alpha_1+H(y)-H(x)\le0,\qquad
 \alpha_2+H(x)-H(y)\le0,
\]
which contradicts $\alpha_1,\alpha_2>0$.

The prescribed profile nevertheless closes the target directly: neither
player can improve his own coordinate.  This example simultaneously proves
that four natural routes---purify the circulation, schedule it, store it in a
common account, or descend to a proper child---are not exhaustive.  The
missing fifth route is precisely Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-closure}.

\subsection{Whole-target separation}

For a finite set of legal terminal or recurrent children with payoff set
$E\subset\RR^I$, exact target matching has the clean convex alternative
\[
 v\in\overline{\co E}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \exists\ell\in(\RR^I)^*:
 \ell(v)>\sup_{w\in E}\ell(w).                  \tag{7.2}
\]
At fixed finite depth, $E$ is compact semialgebraic; over unbounded depths it
may be a nonclosed union, hence closure is necessary.  A separator $\ell$ is
not generally a coordinate functional and need not be owned by one player.
Any theorem that converts (7.2) to a punishment must therefore add an
\emph{owner-custody} or unilateral-realizability hypothesis.

A canonical whole-target separation theorem has been proved under explicit
finite realization, exact scalarized lifting, face closure, and circulation
purification assumptions.  It outputs one of: a coordinate certificate, an
owner defect, a strict face, an owner-realizable charged circulation, or an
omitted child.  Each assumption has a small counterexample if removed.  The
theorem is useful as a typed router; its hypotheses are not consequences of a
generic Bellman germ.

\section{Causal transport and public factorization}

The distinction between ordinary coupling of complete paths and causal
coupling of transition noises is a recurring source of false arguments.
It can be stated exactly.

Let $X$ be a Markov chain on $S$ with kernel $Q$, let
$Z_t=\pi(X_t)\in R$, and let $Y$ be a legal public chain with one stationary
selector $K$, chosen once for the whole path.  For a coupling $\Gamma$, define
the public pair occupation
\[
 M_T^\Gamma(r,r')=
 \sum_{t<T}\Gamma\{Z_{t+1}=r,\,Y_{t+1}=r'\}.
\]
Let $\mathfrak B_T$ be the convex hull of occupations induced by legal
bicausal couplings, and $\mathfrak O_T$ the convex hull induced by arbitrary
couplings of the two complete path laws, allowing legal $K$ in both cases.

\begin{theorem}[Exact occupation rectangularity; \proved]
\label{thm:occupation-rect}
For fixed horizon and initial marginals,
\[
 D_T(c)=\widetilde D_T(c)
 \quad\text{for every }c:R\times R\to[0,1]
\]
if and only if
\[
 \mathfrak B_T=\mathfrak O_T.                   \tag{8.1}
\]
Only the summed one-time pair marginals occur because the same additive cost
is used at every time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both values are lower support functions of the displayed compact convex sets.
Every occupation has total mass $T$.  Equality for costs in $[0,1]$ therefore
implies equality for every real linear direction by affine rescaling.
Compact convex sets are determined by their support functions.
\end{proof}

There is a stronger local sufficient condition.  For a current joint law
$\rho$ of $(X,Y)$, let $\mathsf E^{\rm o}_{Q,K}(\rho)$ be the possible laws of
$(X',Y')$ under arbitrary four-variable couplings having the correct
$(X,X')$ and $(Y,Y')$ marginals.  Let $\mathsf E^{\rm b}_{Q,K}(\rho)$ require,
in addition, a rowwise kernel
\[
 H(x,y,x',y')=\rho(x,y)L_{x,y}(x',y'),
 \qquad L_{x,y}\in\Pi(Q(x),K(y)).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Causal gluing; \proved]
If $\mathsf E^{\rm o}_{Q,K}(\rho)=\mathsf E^{\rm b}_{Q,K}(\rho)$ for every
$\rho$ and legal $K$, then every ordinary path coupling can be replaced by a
legal bicausal coupling with the same law of $(X_t,Y_t)$ at every $t$.
Consequently ordinary and bicausal values agree for arbitrary time-dependent
additive costs.  For full-state, one-step tests with fixed $K$, the condition
is also necessary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take the four-time marginal of an ordinary coupling at time $t$.  Its next-pair
law belongs to $\mathsf E^{\rm o}$.  Replace it by a rowwise coupling in
$\mathsf E^{\rm b}$ with the same next-pair law and iterate.  The Ionescu--Tulcea
construction gives the desired causal pair process.
\end{proof}

Neither lumpability, a preserved fiber posterior, an intertwining
$\Lambda Q=K\Lambda$, filtration immersion, nor a purely fiberwise coupling
condition is sufficient.  The smallest full-state example is
\[
 Q=K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[1mm]2/3&1/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \alpha=(2/5,3/5).
\]
For $c(i,j)=\one_{\{i=j=1\}}$, the ordinary one-step optimum is the Fr\'echet
bound $1/5$, whereas the bicausal optimum is $4/15$.  Yet $\pi$ is the
identity, all fibers are singletons, and the projected law is legal.
For clarity, contemporaneous transition noises are freely coupled in the
bicausal problem.  The gap comes from conditioning on the current pair, not
from independent innovations.  If the initial coupling has
$\rho_{00}=a$, then
\[
 \rho_{01}=\rho_{10}=2/5-a,\qquad \rho_{11}=1/5+a,
\]
and the rowwise Fr\'echet minima are respectively
$1,1/3,1/3,0$.  Hence the bicausal value is
\[
 \min_{0\le a\le2/5}
 \left(a+\frac13(4/5-2a)\right)=\frac4{15}.
\]
An arbitrary complete-path coupling instead chooses the terminal pair
directly and attains terminal mass $1/5$ at $(1,1)$.

Intertwining can even coexist with a linear mismatch gap.  Let
$S=\{a_0,a_1,b\}$, $R=\{A,B\}$, with $a_0,a_1\mapsto A$ and $b\mapsto B$, and
\[
 Q=\begin{array}{c|ccc}
     &a_0&a_1&b\\ \hline
 a_0&1/2&1/2&0\\
 a_1&0&0&1\\
 b&1/2&1/2&0
 \end{array},\qquad
 K(A)=(1/2,1/2),\quad K(B)=(1,0).
\]
With $\Lambda(A)=\tfrac12(\delta_{a_0}+\delta_{a_1})$ and
$\Lambda(B)=\delta_b$, one has $\Lambda Q=K\Lambda$.  At the stationary
posterior the projected complete path is exactly legal, so ordinary mismatch
cost is zero.  Bicausally, whenever $X_t=a_1$ the next public state is $B$,
while a shadow at $A$ must split evenly; stationarity gives mismatch at least
$T/6$.

\subsection{Exact Bellman factorization}

Put $p_s=\pi_*Q(s)$.  If a continuation is public, the bicausal Bellman step
at posterior $\beta$ and public state $y$ is
\[
 \mathcal T_{K,c}h(\beta,y)
 =\sum_s\beta(s)W_{d_h}(p_s,K(y)),\qquad
 d_h(r,y')=c(r,y')+h(y').                       \tag{8.2}
\]
It follows immediately that universal public factorization is much stronger
than lumpability.

\begin{theorem}[Universal public factorization; \proved]
The operator (8.2) is independent of the posterior for every cost,
continuation, legal row, public state, and initial belief if and only if
\[
 \pi_*Q(s)=\pi_*Q(s')\qquad\forall s,s'.         \tag{8.3}
\]
For restricted beliefs, exact equivalence is equality, for every legal row
$k$, of the Minkowski sums
\[
 \sum_s\beta(s)\Pi(p_s,k).
\]
\end{theorem}

For a hidden Markov public process, finite exact predictive memory exists if
and only if the reachable posterior set modulo equality of all future public
word laws is finite.  The minimal number of states is the cardinality of this
probabilistic Myhill--Nerode quotient.  It can be $2^{\Omega(|S|)}$ and can be
infinite.  For the bicausal dual, predictive equivalence must be refined to
equality of all finite-horizon transport signatures.

These transport results align with the modern theory of causal and bicausal
optimal transport \cite{BackhoffBeiglbockLinZalashko2017,Moulos2021}, but the
occupation-rectangularity criterion and the hidden-state counterexamples are
tailored to the uniform-equilibrium obstruction.

\section{Monitoring, accounts, and information rates}

\subsection{Contextual monitoring}

For finitely many predictable public contexts $q$, run one exponential-weights
forecaster in each context.  If context $q$ occurs $N_q$ times and has action
set $A_q$, the pathwise regret against the best fixed contextual policy is
\[
 O\!\left(\sum_q\sqrt{N_q\log|A_q|}\right).      \tag{9.1}
\]
There is no separate context-switch penalty: the clocks are local.  This is a
standard online-learning fact, but its use here is structural.  A public
direction can be monitored whenever it is fixed or belongs to a compressible
finite family; unrestricted opposing directions can cancel and evade a
single e-process.

Virtual queues give a parallel alternative.  For feasible finite affine
constraints, projected queue updates yield $O(\sqrt T)$ cumulative violation.
If infeasible, the normalized queue converges to a Euclidean maximum-margin
Farkas ray, not in general to a simplex-normalized separator.  Under moving
Puiseux constraints the necessary error is the queue-weighted variation, not
the unweighted parameter variation.

\subsection{Linear debt versus quadratic information}

Suppose a deviation is exposed with conditional probability $p_t$ and creates
linear incentive debt $cp_t$.  If exposure is latent, the statistical
distance between prescribed and deviating observations may be only
$O(p_t^2)$.  Hence one may have
\[
 \sum_t p_t=\infty,\qquad \sum_t p_t^2<\infty:   \tag{9.2}
\]
unbounded incentive debt but finite total Kullback--Leibler information.
No purely likelihood-based test can generally pay the debt.  If the exposure
event itself is publicly observed, the information clock becomes linear and
the obstruction disappears.  This separates detection from credibility:
even a valid detector does not say which continuation makes deviation
unprofitable.

\subsection{Uniform energy and finite memory}

Pointwise nonpositive mean on every recurrent class does not by itself give a
uniform finite-prefix bound over an unbounded configuration space.  On a
finite graph, absence of a positive cycle gives a scalar potential and a
bounded pathwise debt; for a finite configuration set the maximal simple
prefix debt is at most the number of configurations minus one after
normalization.  Conversely, a universal $o(T)$ bound for a nonnegative cost
in a finite time-homogeneous system forces every cycle to have zero cost and
therefore yields a bounded potential.  Thus an apparently softer sublinear
account is not more general in a fixed finite arena.

An unbounded quantitative ledger can often be quantized using $O(\log T)$
bits when its magnitude and sensitivity grow polynomially.  This is not a
theorem from local semialgebraicity alone: even a scalar affine push/pop map
can encode an arbitrary binary stack.  A finite-memory claim therefore needs
a contraction, monotonicity, reset, or bounded-distinguishability hypothesis.

\subsection{A universal analytic calendar}

For every fixed finite family of Puiseux error exponents, the calendar
\[
 b_k=2^k,\qquad
 \lambda_k=\exp\!\left(-\frac{k}{\log(k+e)}\right)           \tag{9.3}
\]
separates polynomial mixing debts from the block lengths and drives every
positive Puiseux error to zero.  The same construction works simultaneously
for finitely many coordinates and contexts when each uses its own local
clock.  The result schedules an already proved analytic ledger; it does not
create the ledger, establish delivery, or supply punishment credibility.
In particular, it cannot undo an irreversible higher-order leak or make
incompatible reduced-class fluxes implementable by one global calendar.

\section{Finite trees, stopping, and endogenous targets}

Once genuine lower-ranked children are available, much of their composition
is understood.  The hard part is that a child must contain an actual strategy,
a full payoff target, a legal entry map, and historywise unilateral bounds.

\subsection{Backward Nash on a public tree}

Consider a finite public tree.  Each nonterminal node carries a finite
one-shot game whose payoff for a joint action is the expected continuation
vector at its children; each leaf carries an arbitrary terminal payoff vector.

\begin{theorem}[Finite public terminal Nash system; \leanchecked]
\label{thm:terminal-nash}
Backward induction may select a mixed Nash equilibrium at every node.  The
resulting public behavior profile is subgame perfect on the tree.  For every
player and every node, the prescribed continuation payoff equals the supremum
over all unilateral behavior deviations on the remaining tree.  In
particular, prescribed and worst-unilateral terminal potentials coincide
exactly, not merely up to an error.
\end{theorem}

This theorem makes the parent target endogenous: it is the root payoff of the
backward-Nash system.  If a proof needs a preassigned target $v$, a separate
target-transport theorem is still required.  Backward induction cannot force
its root value to equal an externally chosen analytic endpoint.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-depth child composition; \leanchecked, conditional]
\label{thm:fixed-depth}
Suppose every leaf of a finite public selector tree carries an all-accuracy
adaptive child certificate.  Suppose public suffix rebasing commutes with
one-step extension, terminal child targets are the continuation payoffs used
by the backward-Nash system, and the selector has the required target anchors
and unilateral safety.  Then the spliced parent profile has an adaptive
certificate.  The finite prefix contributes only a bounded potential charge.
\end{theorem}

The checked implementation also has approximate-target and ranked-child
variants.  Their value is compositional: once the leaf data are genuine, no
new long-run incentive argument is needed at the parent.

\subsection{Randomized recurrent children}

Let a public selection phase stop by time $L$, under prescribed play and
under every unilateral deviation, and select a publicly observed child $J$.
Assume child $c$ has target $v^c$ and a continuation which is uniformly
$\varepsilon$-equilibrium after every rebased entry at which it can be invoked.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded randomized-child composition; \proved]
\label{thm:random-child}
If under prescribed selection
\[
 \EE[v^J]=v,                                      \tag{10.1}
\]
and every unilateral deviation during selection satisfies
\[
 \EE[v_i^J]\le v_i+\eta,                         \tag{10.2}
\]
then the composite expected Cesàro payoff converges to $v$ and every
unilateral asymptotic gain is at most $\varepsilon+\eta$.  More precisely, if
$A_i(n),B_i(n)=o(n)$ are uniform prescribed and deviation moduli for the
children, then for $N>L$ the additional selector loss is bounded by
\[
 \frac{2L+L\max_c|v_i^c|+\max_{N-L\le n\le N}B_i(n)}{N}
\]
up to an inessential normalization of the first two constants.  A single
behavior deviation used before and after the stopping time causes no extra
loss.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Conditioning argument]
On a terminal selection history $H_\tau=h$ with child $c$, pull the global
deviator back along the public suffix-rebasing map.  Commutation with one-step
extension makes the pullback a legal child behavior strategy and identifies
the conditional continuation law.  If private information survives the
selector, condition also on that information; after forgetting it one obtains
a mixture of legal child deviations, and the child inequality is affine.
The prefix has at most $L$ bounded-payoff terms.  Condition on
$\mathcal F_\tau$, apply the child modulus to $N-\tau$, average, and use
(10.1)--(10.2).
\end{proof}

Root condition (10.2) is sufficient for semantic composition.  A
historywise public-phase certificate requires the same inequality after every
public prefix.  If all public information determining $\tau$ and $J$ is
generated by an action-independent public kernel, the law of $J$ is invariant
under unilateral deviation and (10.2) holds with $\eta=0$ whenever (10.1)
does.  Action-independence of only the physical state is not enough if the
public stopped history records actions.

\subsection{Public stopping and entry debt}

For a public stopping time $\tau$, the continuation of a global behavior
strategy after the stopped history is again a legal behavior strategy in the
rebased child.  This yields exact factorization of stopped cylinder laws and
is checked for bounded stopping.  Unbounded stopping requires three separate
controls:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the nonentry event;
\item uniform integrability or a quantitative tail bound for the prefix;
\item a child modulus uniform over all stopped entry histories.
\end{enumerate}
Confusing positive reachability, almost-sure entry, and positive probability
in every recurrent class leads to invalid proofs.

Let the target $w(h)$ be harmonic on declared recurrent classes, and suppose a
stopped child gives a two-sided prescribed estimate and a unilateral upper cap
after every legal entry.  Then the deviation gain from delaying or changing
entry differs from the explicit target-entry debt by $o(N)$.  This provides a
precise reduction: the strategic question is whether that entry debt is
nonpositive or can be charged, not whether optional stopping mysteriously
preserves the target.

\begin{remark}
A semantic continuation after a stopping time need not be an adaptive
certificate.  It is sometimes only a uniform-payoff/deviation-cap witness.
The compiler must therefore have a distinct semantic-terminal type rather than
pretending that every semantic leaf carries historywise ledgers.
\end{remark}

\section{Approachability, regeneration, and account discharge}

\subsection{Moving-bias Markov approachability}

For vector charges in a controlled finite Markov chain, a Blackwell-type
approachability rule \cite{Blackwell1956} can be combined with a moving Poisson
bias.  Literal observation of the required vector charge may fail when it
depends on hidden transition noise.  Under perfect monitoring, freezing the
bias over blocks and paying the mixing boundary gives a robust
$O(T^{-1/3})$ rate.  If a common bounded bias is available, or the bias varies
with a regularity modulus strong enough to make total variation summable, the
usual $O(T^{-1/2})$ rate is recovered.  Any descent event used in this
construction must be measurable when the response is selected; an analytic
classification revealed only after future play is not a causal strategy.

\subsection{Regeneration and support automata}

For a finite family of public kernels, a common open-loop regeneration word
exists exactly when the induced subset automaton has a word sending every
possible support into a common singleton or declared regeneration support.
If it exists, a shortest word has length at most $2^{|S|}-1$.  A feedback
regeneration policy is strictly more powerful and is characterized by winning
in the corresponding finite subset game.  Failure of regeneration produces a
recurrent subset cycle.  It does \emph{not} automatically give analytic rank
descent, owner purity, or a credible punishment.

\subsection{Path-complete reset accounting}

A quantitative Streett-style account system consists of finitely many modes,
debts, and reset events.  A necessary condition for uniform closure is that
every reset used linearly often has a payer whose accumulated credit is also
linear, while every unpaid reset is used $o(T)$ times.  On a fixed finite
time-homogeneous graph, this condition reduces to a small-gain/cycle test and
hence to a bounded scalar potential.  More elaborate path-complete accounts
are useful only because the public mode, target, or scale changes; those
changes must themselves have a finite or sublinear budget.

\section{Full support, deflation, and canonical rank}

Support loss is an attractive source of induction, but full support is exactly
where it stops.  The program uses a lexicographic rank with a harmonic
coordinate and a support/operational coordinate.

\subsection{Operational deflation}

In an actual analytic full-support branch, let a strict leading drift identify
columns whose use would incur a positive charge.  Delete those operational
columns and charge every subsequent use to the leading-drift account.

\begin{theorem}[Full-support operational deflation; \leanchecked]
\label{thm:deflation}
Under the checked full-support leading-drift hypotheses, the deleted
operational set is nonempty, the third component of the lexicographic rank
strictly decreases, and both the total mass and the realized number of uses of
deleted columns are sublinear on the analytic calendar.
\end{theorem}

This is genuine finite progress.  It does not prove that the remaining action
profile delivers the original whole target, that entry is legal after every
deviation, or that the response is credible.  These are independent seams.

At full support with zero leading pairing, the average-reward LP yields an
exact constant $\gamma$ and a bias $h$ satisfying adjusted row inequalities.
Full support can equalize every used row at a \emph{positive} $\gamma$; neither
support, valuation, nor graph structure forces terminal closure.  The mixed
Farkas alternative is also sharp: each player's rows can be separately
feasible while the joint positive obstruction is genuinely mixed-owner.

\subsection{Canonical strategic nodes}

A recursive node cannot be identified only by the root state and a numerical
rank.  It must include the legal suffix type, full target, processed harmonic
span, active support face, owner/account data, and the public rebasing map.
For a rooted suffix type $u$, define the semantic rank
\[
 \operatorname{srank}(u)=\dim(D_u\cap\ker L_u),
\]
where $D_u$ is the current neutral-direction space and $L_u$ the complete
target/occupation constraint map.  Define global rank as the maximum semantic
rank over \emph{all} reachable rooted suffix types, not merely those reached
under prescribed play.

\begin{proposition}[Rank invariance under coherent rebasing; \proved]
If a suffix correspondence is two-sided and total on every reachable object,
and carries objectwise neutral-space isomorphisms commuting naturally with
the target maps, then global rank is invariant under rebasing.  A root-only
correspondence is insufficient: a deviation can reach a suffix with larger
neutral dimension.
\end{proposition}

The naturality clauses are not decorative.  Without them an abstract vector
space isomorphism preserves dimension but not the strategic meaning of
support deletion, target transport, ownership, or account discharge.

\subsection{The finite processed-data alternative}

For a fixed processed span, finite linear algebra gives one of four outputs:
a valid ledger in the span, a new independent harmonic direction, a reachable
class with nonzero mean, or a finite incompatibility certificate.  Harmonic
data alone do not decide which output is strategically useful.  Target rows,
public mode, ownership, and legal entry must be included in the processed
datum.  A rank-decreasing realization theorem has been proved under six
explicit hypotheses: joint behavior realization, legal entry, complete target
transport, one-sided deviation ledgers, exact rebasing, and rank-faithful
closure.  Removing any of these merely moves the conjecture into the premise.

\section{Zero-pairing algebra and its strategic limits}

At a full-support endpoint, repeated vanishing of the first Bellman pairings
leads to a finite jet-extension problem.  The algebraic part can be stated
cleanly.

\begin{theorem}[Finite owner-history zero-pairing trichotomy; \proved]
\label{thm:zero-pairing}
For finite analytic row, occupation, and target data, iteratively adjoin the
first nonzero jet not annihilated by the processed constraint space.  At each
step exactly one of the following occurs:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a first nonzero oriented pairing is exposed;
\item the neutral space strictly decreases;
\item every remaining charge is a circulation-coboundary on the stabilized
      support.
\end{enumerate}
The alternatives have finite linear certificates.  There is no bound on the
first nonzero analytic order depending only on the state and action counts.
\end{theorem}

A strengthened normalization schema can attach whole-target data, owner
history, account orientation, a proper face, a typed zero-pair, or an omitted
child to the corresponding algebraic output.  A verified normalization
theorem of precisely this form is available as best-effort mathematics.  It
does not establish that the output is reachable, credible, or recursively
closing.  In particular, a circulation-coboundary identity is not a strategy.

The most honest full-support alternative currently has typed outputs for
direct harmless closure, owner-pure response, processed account, proper-face
child, zero-pairing continuation, and mixed incompatibility.  The alternative
is exhaustive as finite algebra.  Routing every output to a uniform strategy
remains open.

\section{Finite schedulers, plateaux, and proof-carrying recursion}

\subsection{Typed finite public scheduling}

Suppose several local realization contexts prescribe compatible-looking
state--action occupations but use different public modes, owner histories, or
target rebasings.  A finite scheduler is not merely a convex combination.  It
is a Markov lift on an expanded public state space whose stationary flow
projects to every prescribed context and whose reachable recurrent support is
entered from the designated root.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-template schedulability; \proved]
\label{thm:scheduler}
For a fixed finite public-mode template, exact simultaneous realization is
equivalent to feasibility of a finite system consisting of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item stochastic row and public-flow equations on the lifted arena;
\item projection equations for every typed context occupation;
\item positive reachability, or exact transient-routing flow, from the entry;
\item totality of every unilateral row visible to a deviator;
\item target superharmonicity and owner-history balance on the expanded state;
\item commutation of suffix, target, account, and analytic-data diagrams.
\end{enumerate}
For algebraic input, existence is decidable by quantifier elimination.  Every
linear subfailure has a Farkas or flow certificate.
\end{theorem}

Public flow balance alone is insufficient, as are separate stationarity in
each context, convex-hull membership of row laws, owner homogeneity, or
reachability under a different scheduler.  Mode erasure is especially
dangerous: a deviator observes the mode and can condition on it.  All
unilateral occupation tests must be rerun on the expanded state.

There is no bound on the number of required modes depending only on the
dimension of the occupation vectors.  One-dimensional families can force
arbitrarily many modes, and an irrational prescribed frequency can rule out an
exact rational finite scheduler.  A finite mode bound follows under a genuine
public-resampling hypothesis that regenerates context independently at
declared times.

\subsection{Canonical plateaux}

Rank need not decrease along every local rewrite.  A finite semantic signature
may instead produce a strongly connected plateau of mutually rebased nodes.
The correct progress measure is
\[
 \widehat\rho=(\rho,h_{\mathrm{nr}},h_{\mathrm{pl}}),
\]
where $\rho$ is the semantic restriction rank, $h_{\mathrm{nr}}$ is the height
of the neutral-restriction lattice, and $h_{\mathrm{pl}}$ is the condensation
height of the finite plateau graph.

\begin{theorem}[Coherent plateau alternative; \conditional]
\label{thm:plateau}
Assume a finite atlas-complete semantic signature; coherent, invertible suffix
transport on the semantic quotient; natural target and germ rebasing; a
realizable common scheduler; and zero owner/account holonomy on a terminal
plateau strongly connected component.  Then the whole component, treated as
one object, yields one of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item semantic closure;
\item a target-calibrated terminal public system;
\item a completely discharged realized account;
\item an exit with strictly smaller $\widehat\rho$;
\item a typed recurrent obstruction.
\end{enumerate}
The refined measure is invariant under continuation-complete strategic
bisimulation, conservative memory covers, exact target rebasing, and coherent
germ restart.
\end{theorem}

The hypotheses are the content.  Continuous target families and unbounded
memory give infinite plateau graphs; mixed-owner holonomy and incompatible
realization contexts invalidate closure.  The theorem consumes scheduler and
holonomy certificates; it does not prove them.

\subsection{Effective audit}

A well-founded codomain does not establish that declared child edges decrease
rank.  Likewise, a finite list of rule names does not establish guard
exhaustiveness.  An effective audit is possible only relative to a decidable
or proof-carrying certificate theory.

\begin{theorem}[Effective progress-completeness; \proved, conditional]
\label{thm:audit}
Let a local rule registry have effective canonicalization, finitely many
decidable or proof-carrying guards, an exhaustive guard cover, an eight-gate
child verifier (strategy, credibility, target, entry, rebasing, account,
rank, and certificate provenance), grounded terminal rules, and checkable
strict-rank or plateau contracts.  Then a finite algorithm constructs the
status-refined obstruction graph, detects every open vertex and every
unjustified cycle, verifies finite branching, and emits either:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a proof-carrying progress-completeness certificate; or
\item a finite obstruction identifying the first failed guard, child gate,
      rank edge, terminal justification, or accuracy quantifier.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem cannot turn an unrouted obstruction into progress.  A trivial
self-child passes superficial interface checks and proves why intrinsic rank
must be audited.  Allowing an arbitrary claimed modulus in a certificate makes
total audit undecidable by encoding nonhalting; hence the proof-carrying
restriction is necessary.

\subsection{From recursion to a finite response tree}

\begin{theorem}[Occurrence-sensitive finite unfolding; \proved, conditional]
\label{thm:unfolding}
Let every nonterminal occurrence have finitely many children, each carrying a
proof of strict decrease in a well-founded rank, and let every terminal
occurrence carry one of the certified terminal types.  Then the path-tagged
unfolding from a root is finite.  It can be compiled to an explicit finite
public response tree.  Choosing the desired accuracy before unfolding and
combining terminal systems by backward Nash and stopped-history rebasing
produces the root certificate supplied by the local composition theorems.
\end{theorem}

Canonical nodes and occurrences must be distinguished.  The same canonical
node may occur with different inherited targets, ledgers, or error budgets;
merging them is sound only after an explicit coherence proof.  Finite
branching plus well-founded descent gives finite height by induction, while
well-foundedness alone does not.  Most importantly, the quantifiers must be
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\quad
 \text{construct the entire finite tree and profile}\quad
 \forall N\ge N_\varepsilon,
\]
not a horizon-dependent tree chosen after $N$ is known.

\section{No-go results that delimit the program}

The project has accumulated a collection of counterexamples which are as
important as the positive lemmas.

\subsection{Credibility is not detection}

A public score with positive drift under a deviating row can identify a
statistical inconsistency.  It need not produce a continuation that lowers the
deviator's payoff.  A credible response requires local lower and upper payoff
inequalities and a deviation-envelope inequality after every response
history.  When these inequalities and sublinear residual ledgers are supplied,
a finite enforcement compiler gives an adaptive certificate.  Without them,
the detector is only evidence.

\subsection{Positive circulation is not target transport}

A positive recurrent circulation can move the entire payoff vector away from
the endpoint target, as the mixed-owner example in Section~7 shows.  Even an
owner-pure positive circulation need not preserve all coordinates.  The
target must be carried as data and checked at every child.  A harmonic
direction is a separating functional, not a replacement payoff vector.

\subsection{Entry is class-relative}

Positive probability of reaching a set, almost-sure eventual entry, and
positive stationary mass in every entered recurrent class are inequivalent.
A global circulation may live entirely in a class which the declared public
response never reaches.  Exact finite MDP linear programs characterize
deviation-robust reachability and nonentry for one player; profiles that solve
these entry problems separately for different players may be incompatible.

\subsection{Finite phases do not exhaust adaptive play}

For a fixed bounded phase template, existence of a semialgebraic certificate
is a first-order theory of the reals problem and is decidable.  This does not
give completeness over all public strategies.  Observer quotients may be
exponential or infinite, mode requirements are unbounded, and the Big Match
requires a genuinely adaptive schedule.  Thus exhaustive search over bounded
templates can certify a discovered construction but cannot settle the general
conjecture.

\subsection{Absolute terminal control destroys legitimate branch cancellation}

Consider an action-independent game with an initial state $s_0$ which moves
with probability $1/2$ to an absorbing state of reward $+1$ and with
probability $1/2$ to an absorbing state of reward $-1$.  Actions are duplicate
and a trivial uniform equilibrium delivers the ex-ante target $0$.

A tempting stronger certificate asks for a historywise target-relative drift
inequality with a nonnegative account $C$ and potential $\Phi$, together with
\[
 \EE\bigl[C_N+|\Phi_N-\Phi_0|\bigr]=o(N).
\]
On the positive absorbing branch the local inequality forces
$C_N-\Phi_N$ to grow linearly.  Since that branch has probability $1/2$, the
displayed absolute remainder is impossible.  The negative branch cancels it
in expected payoff but cannot cancel an absolute value.

Thus this stronger local certificate is not a universal normal form, even for
action-independent transitions.  For the elementary upper-payoff telescope it
would be enough to control the signed remainder
\[
 \EE\bigl[(C_N-C_0)-(\Phi_N-\Phi_0)\bigr]=o(N).
\]
Whether such signed control can be made stable under the stopping, rebasing,
and recursive operations required by the general proof is a separate
question.  The counterexample does not affect
Theorem~\ref{thm:verifier}, whose expectation-level monotonicity already
preserves the public-branch cancellation.

\subsection{Analytic self-similarity}

A target-coherent analytic leaf may restart into a leaf of the same semantic
rank.  The Big Match is the model phenomenon: the successful strategy changes
its testing rate using accumulated evidence rather than descending through a
finite atlas.  A semantic leaf theorem is useful only if it supplies an actual
accuracy-indexed strategy with restart and stopped-history guarantees.  A
record saying “this leaf is semantically solvable” without such a theorem is
equivalent to the root problem.

\section{Known terminal classes and machine-checked stress tests}

The general bridge is absent, but several terminal classes are complete.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Zero-sum games.}  The Mertens--Neyman criterion supplies the
      adaptive scalar strategy once the discounted value has the necessary
      regularity.  The present formal development checks an abstract version
      of the criterion but does not yet derive its deepest tracking estimate
      from the general analytic germ.
\item \textbf{Absorbing and single-state classes.}  Uniform equilibrium
      constructions are checked for the corresponding finite special cases.
\item \textbf{Action-independent transitions.}  Abel--Cesàro delivery and
      static equilibrium selection reduce the problem to a finite public
      terminal system; this class is checked.
\item \textbf{The Big Match.}  A Blackwell--Ferguson history-dependent
      witness and its uniform payoff are checked.  A separate theorem proves
      that no calendar-time state-Markov profile witnesses the value.  This is
      a precise formal stress test for any proposed finite-memory closure.
\item \textbf{Single-controller and related LP classes.}  The strategic
      compiler is checked conditional on the appropriate average-reward LP
      input.
\item \textbf{Pure externality cycle.}  The two-state mixed-owner example and
      its holonomy obstruction are formalized as a keeper counterexample.
\end{itemize}

These special cases validate the certificate API and several leaf types.
They do not establish that every analytic endpoint reduces to one of those
terminal classes.

\section{The remaining mathematical program}

The gap can now be stated without reference to implementation.

\begin{problem}[Endogenous germ and target selection with realization]
\label{prob:master}
Given a finite stochastic game and an initial state, select an analytic Bellman
germ and endpoint leaf from the discounted-equilibrium correspondence, select
a payoff vector $w$---not necessarily the germ endpoint---and construct one of
the following from finite or proof-carrying source data:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item an actual public profile satisfying the hypotheses of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-closure} at target $w$;
\item a credible public response with complete transport of $w$ and a
      discharged account;
\item finitely many legal children, together with proved target bridges from
      $w$ to their declared complete-vector targets, whose canonical rank is
      strictly smaller;
\item an accuracy-indexed semantic strategy with restartable,
      stopped-history uniform bounds and payoff $w$.
\end{enumerate}
Prove that germ selection, target selection, and the alternatives cover every
game and commute with canonical rebasing.  The target-selection stage must handle
\S\ref{sec:sorin-target}: it must not attempt to preserve the discounted
endpoint $(1/2,2/3)$ in Sorin's game.
\end{problem}

Problem~\ref{prob:master} is still too large to attack as one lemma, but it
splits into scoped questions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{P\arabic*.},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item \textbf{Root germ and target selection.}  Choose a useful analytic
branch and define a finite or proof-carrying correspondence of strategically
implementable candidate targets.  Prove it is nonempty for every initial state
and show how analytic endpoint data enters the selection without forcing
equality to the endpoint.  It must select a
point of (6.8), rather than (6.7), in Sorin's example.
\item \textbf{Whole-target lifting after selection.}  Characterize when an analytic scalar
separator has a player-owned unilateral lift, and route failure to a strict
face or a harmlessness certificate.
\item \textbf{Credibility.}  From the first nonzero owned Bellman jet,
construct an actual response profile with historywise deviation envelopes, or
give a finite incompatibility certificate consumed by another route.
\item \textbf{Payoff compilation.}  Derive the shifted inequality (6.2) from
the analytic germ, including invisible unused rows and moving target terms.
\item \textbf{Prescribed delivery.}  The coupled-scale Abel reduction and the
finite-mode feedback criteria are now known.  Lift them to an accuracy-indexed
game-theoretic scheduler with endogenous public randomness, complete-vector
target error, and uniform unilateral-deviation caps.  Exact one-policy
realization cannot be imposed: Example~\ref{ex:multiscale-nonclosed} is
unattainable but arbitrarily approximable.
\item \textbf{Robust entry.}  Build one public selector satisfying all
players' entry and nonentry requirements, rather than separate incompatible
MDP selectors.
\item \textbf{Full-support zero pairing.}  Turn every algebraic output of
Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-pairing} into direct closure, a credible account, or a
proper whole-target child.
\item \textbf{Scheduler/holonomy.}  Decide and consume the incompatible-mode
and mixed-owner holonomy outputs of Theorems~\ref{thm:scheduler} and
\ref{thm:plateau}.
\item \textbf{Target-coherent child analysis.}  Show that every recursive child
admits analytic source data and a proved bridge to its declared child target.
Equality between a restarted discounted endpoint and the child target may be a
useful special case, but cannot be imposed as a universal invariant.
\item \textbf{Adaptive semantic supply.}  Generalize the scalar
Mertens--Neyman adaptive mechanism to the multiplayer, vector-target,
deviation-specific setting, or prove that every leaf requiring it has already
decreased a canonical rank.
\item \textbf{Joint completeness.}  With the preceding outputs as concrete
data, prove exhaustive guarded coverage and apply the occurrence-sensitive
finite-tree compiler.
\end{enumerate}

No local item says “prove the uniform-equilibrium conjecture,” yet their joint
solution would do so: Theorem~\ref{thm:germ} and the endpoint atlas supply a
nonempty source correspondence, P1 selects a germ and admissible root target,
P2--P10 supply a
terminal or strictly lower children with target bridges, P11 gives a finite
tree, and Theorem~\ref{thm:verifier} gives the uniform equilibrium.
Conversely, negative answers are valuable only if their obstruction has a
declared consumer.  An opaque incompatibility does not close a game.

\subsection{The seven-seam acceptance test}

Future claims of a generic leaf closure should be evaluated against seven
questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Where are the analytic branch and root target selected, and why is the
      latter strategically implementable rather than merely a discounted
      endpoint?
\item Where is the actual behavior profile?
\item Why is the response safe against every unilateral continuation?
\item Once selected, where is the complete payoff vector anchored and
      transported?
\item Why can the response be entered and rebased after the histories in
      question?
\item What strictly decreases, or which realized account is completely
      discharged?
\item Where is the direct sound-certificate, semantic deviation-cap, or
      semantic-terminal theorem, without a “closer” assumption returning the
      same object?
\end{enumerate}
At present the generic score is zero closed nonsemantic atlas leaves out of
thirteen under the existing bookkeeping.  Sorin's example shows that this
cannot be read as thirteen discounted endpoints waiting to be preserved: the
root target-selection interface is itself missing.  The correct interpretation
is not that the project has stalled completely: the analytic source, semantic
verifier, composition calculus, and many boundary theorems are substantial.
It is that the remaining difficulty has been localized and corrected, rather
than solved.

\section{Conclusion}

The central conceptual invariant emerging from this work is not a single
scalar potential.  It has a germ-and-target-selection front end followed by a
proof-carrying public continuation object coupling four kinds of conservation:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{endogenous selection of a useful germ and implementable root target}\\[1mm]
\downarrow\\[-1mm]
\text{whole-target transport}\\
\text{unilateral incentive supermartingales}\\
\text{legal stopped-history rebasing}\\
\text{well-founded support/harmonic progress or realized account discharge}.
\end{array}}
\]
Analytic Bellman germs generate precise infinitesimal data, but the endpoint
need not be a uniform payoff, and germ selection, target selection, causality,
ownership, and delivery are not consequences of analytic regularity.
Occupation LPs can close a surprisingly broad strategically inert branch
without a common potential.  Finite public trees can compose genuine children
without further asymptotic ingenuity.  Causal transport and mixed-owner
examples show exactly why several elegant shortcuts fail.

The open conjecture therefore sits at a narrower but still formidable bridge:
select useful analytic source data and an implementable target, then
build a continuation object which coherently transports that target.
The merit of the present framework is
falsifiability.  A proposed solution can now fail at a named finite seam, and
a successful local theorem can be incorporated without pretending that the
other seams have disappeared.
