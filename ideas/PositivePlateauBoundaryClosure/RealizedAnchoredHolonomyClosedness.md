# Realized anchored holonomy should be closed—or fail informatively

| Status | Provenance | Formalization | Next discriminant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PARTIAL`, maturity `M+L/I` | E50 plus `QuittingBoundaryHolonomy`; proof-mining §83; Q133 | fixed-cutoff full lift compact/closed; literal unbounded-cost lift impossible | Prove game-stage tightness, or build a compact infinity/stopping-law chart with a separately bounded decoder |

Let `H_m` be holonomies of anchored middle blocks drawn from calibrated finite
minimizers whose lengths diverge. The scalar summaries are precompact. The
central question is whether every convergent subsequence has a limit that
retains enough finite labels or limiting stopping-law data to map the reverse
terminal packet chart into the forward seam chart and admit chronological
splicing.

A positive theorem may require support/active-branch labels, iterated hazard
scales, or a compact stopping-law/Young-measure coordinate. A decisive negative
example is equally valuable: two calibrated block families with identical
limiting `(B,P,A,T,χ)` data but incompatible source roots, terminal marks, or
splice behavior identifies exactly what the scalar summary forgot.

Compactness of the ambient coefficient box is not closedness of the realized
subset. Nor does a closed relaxation suffice unless every accepted limit
decodes to an executable strategy or every nonrealizable point has a uniform
separating margin consumable by debt descent.

The fixed-cutoff part is now settled in production Lean by
`QuittingBoundaryHolonomyCompactness`.  For every cutoff, the graph retaining
the complete exact zero-boundary Nash--Bellman path and every nonempty
subblock holonomy is compact and closed.  The calibrated refinement is even
finite at fixed terminal index and retains, in one common state, the selected
minimizer, one-hot owner code, full marked action, exact-D entry and exit, the
preterminal survival, the final marked atom, and all playerwise holonomy
coordinates.  Every production `QuittingAnchoredBoundaryBlock` maps into this
lift without relaxation.  Thus no fixed-length support/provenance defect is
left to blame.

There is also a sharp Q133 fence.  Any compact lift that retains literal game
length as an ordinary natural-valued coordinate has a uniform length bound;
an unbounded family in `ℕ × X` is noncompact regardless of how compact its
projection to `X` is.  Therefore the maximal unconditional closed subclass is
the bounded-cutoff/fixed-last class.  Extending the length coordinate by an
infinite point may restore topological compactness, but it destroys the
bounded game-stage cost needed by Q133's repair-or-drop conclusion unless a
separate decoder proves a uniform finite realization bound.

The exact additional hypothesis is consequently **uniform middle-length
tightness** on the positive-plateau component.  No such bound currently
follows from calibration, E50, or the scalar coefficient box; the selected
cutoffs are precisely allowed to escape.  The remaining question is not
ordinary fixed-word closedness.  It is whether the escaping middle admits a
strategically executable compact infinity/stopping-law chart with bounded
finite decoding, or instead yields a calibrated incompatibility family.
