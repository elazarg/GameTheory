## Simon's structure

From *The challenge of non-zero-sum stochastic games*, IJGT 45(1) 191–204
(2016), §"Quitting games". Writing `W = ℝⁿ \ {s : s_j > 1 for all j}`, so a
vector is *inside* `W` exactly when some coordinate is at most `1`:

> Looking at any player `j` who quits with positive probability, it does so
> because at least one other player also quits with positive probability. The
> same is true of that other player. The most likely induced structure is a
> **cycle of players who inspire each other to quit** with positive
> probability. As we would not want such a cycle to include all the players
> (as that would suggest either a serious difficulty ever landing into the
> subset `D` or a tendency for vectors outside of `W` to stay outside of `W`),
> we should look at quitting games with **proper subsets** of such cycles of
> players.

and the forecast this yields:

> This gives some clue why it is difficult to find quitting game
> counter-examples with only four players, as any directed graph on four
> vertices has a very limited structure of proper cycles. Only with five
> vertices can we find a good structure of proper cycles. However ... we would
> expect a counter-example, should it exist, to have at least six players. We
> think that eight player quitting games are much more promising, but because
> of the extreme number of cases generated by higher dimensions we expect a
> confirmation of a counter-example to be possible only with the help of
> computers.

So the object is: a **digraph on the player set**, edge `j → k` when `k`
quitting with positive probability is what makes `j` willing to quit; the
relevant feature is its **cycle structure**; and the constraint that separates
plausible from implausible counterexamples is that the cycles be **proper**.

## What this repository has, and does not have

**Has, generically.** `Math/BoundedDiscrepancyCirculation.lean` defines a
directed multigraph `EdgeGraph V E` with explicit edge identities and an
inductive `Walk` retaining the edge list, with length and concatenation.
`QuittingMarkedFenceIteration.lean` carries the label-walk pigeonhole lemmas
(`exists_finiteLabelWalk_good_or_repeat` and relatives) that the holonomy lane
uses. Mathlib's `SimpleGraph` is undirected and is not the right tool; the
home-grown `EdgeGraph` is.

**Does not have: any digraph indexed by players.** The single exception is
`QuittingRefutedRouteFences.lean`, which defines a `Blocks` relation between
coordinates and proves `blockingDigraph_isTwoCycle` — but only for `ι = Bool`,
and only as a *fence* recording that a two-phase route is unsolvable. There is
no general inspiration relation, no cycle structure on it, and no theorem
connecting either to the weight.

**So the approach is genuinely missing**, and the infrastructure to express it
is already present one directory over.

## The identification worth testing

Let `M_{ij} = r_i({j}) − r_i({i})` be the normalized solo matrix of
[`README`](README.md): row `i` the payoff receiver, column `j` the sole
quitter, zero diagonal. Define the **sign digraph** of `M` by putting an edge
`i → j` when `M_{ij} < 0` — player `i` strictly prefers quitting alone to `j`
quitting alone, so `j`'s quitting is *bad* for `i` and does not by itself
persuade `i` to stay.

Two pieces of evidence that this is Simon's graph.

1. **Ashkenazi-Golan–Krasikov–Rainer–Solan's `Q̄` criterion at `n = 3` is
   literally a statement about cycles.** Their Remark 5.3 says a `3×3` matrix
   is `Q̄` iff, up to permutation, either the strictly lower triangle is
   nonnegative — equivalently some **column** is nonnegative with zero
   diagonal, a *sink*: one player whose solo quit no one minds — or the sign
   pattern is the **directed 3-cycle**

   ```
   ( 0  ≤  ≥ )
   ( ≥  0  ≤ )
   ( ≤  ≥  0 )
   ```

   with `det M ≥ 0`. So at three players the published sufficient condition
   reads: *a sink, or a directed cycle with a determinant side condition.*
   That is a graph-theoretic criterion wearing linear-algebra clothes.

2. **The canonical cyclic game realizes the cyclic pattern.** Flesch–Thuijsman–
   Vrieze's cubic game — the standard witness that stationary strategies do not
   suffice, whose equilibrium is a length-3 cycle in which each player quits in
   turn — has exactly that sign pattern, with `det M = 7 > 0`. The perturbed
   `Γ_η` inherits it, because `η` moves only the pair rows and `M` sees only
   the singleton rows.

If the identification holds, three things follow, each checkable:

- The `Q̄` condition generalizes to a **combinatorial** criterion on the sign
  digraph plus determinant side conditions on its cycles.
- The residual class of [`README`](README.md) — `M` a `Q`-matrix with some
  proper principal minor failing `Q` — becomes a statement about **proper
  subgraphs carrying the cycle structure**, which is Simon's "proper subsets of
  such cycles of players", arrived at from an entirely different direction.
- Simon's player-count forecast becomes a **computable screen**: enumerate
  digraphs on `n ≤ 8` vertices up to isomorphism, keep those whose proper-cycle
  structure is rich enough, and test only the sign patterns they permit. He
  expected machine search to be necessary; this would say what to search.

## Falsifiers and wrong turns

- **The identification may simply be false.** "Inspires to quit" is a condition
  at a one-stage equilibrium against a *continuation vector*, and `M` is a
  condition on the weight alone. The edge relation may depend on the
  continuation and not factor through `M`. Test this **first**, on the weights
  already in the tree, before building anything.
- The edge orientation above (`i → j` when `M_{ij} < 0`) is a guess. Simon's
  relation runs from the player who is persuaded to the player who persuades;
  check which direction, and whether the right condition is a strict sign, a
  weak sign, or something involving the pair rows.
- **`M` cannot see the multi-quitter rows.** That is exactly right for
  continuous equilibria, which put no mass on simultaneous quits, and wrong for
  anything else. Simon's inspiration structure is about players quitting
  *simultaneously with positive probability*, which is where the pair rows
  live. This is the most likely place the identification breaks, and it should
  be the first thing checked.
- Do not read Simon's forecast as a theorem. It is an argued expectation, in a
  survey, about where counterexamples would be found — useful as a search
  prior and as a fence on the refutation lane, not as a result.
- Do not build digraph infrastructure before the identification is decided. If
  it fails, the right object is something else and the effort is wasted.
