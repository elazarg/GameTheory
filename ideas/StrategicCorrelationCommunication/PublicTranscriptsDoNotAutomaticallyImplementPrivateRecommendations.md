## Intuition

A public seed tells every player the same realized signal. Conditional on that common signal, ordinary simultaneous play generated by independent private randomization has a product action law. A private recommendation device instead gives player `i` only their own recommendation. Obedience can depend on the posterior distribution of other recommendations conditional on that private message; revealing the whole recommendation vector may destroy that posterior and the incentive constraint it supports.

Therefore “sample the correlated distribution publicly, then play the sampled action profile” is not an automatic implementation argument. It changes what each player knows before acting.

Solan--Vieille autonomous correlation is a third, more structured object: private, history-dependent current recommendations combined with delayed public disclosure. It is not merely an exogenous public sunspot, and neither object is automatically reproduced by a jointly controlled public lottery.

## Mathematical interface

For a public signal `Z`, ordinary behavioral play has conditional joint law

\[
\mathcal L(A\mid Z=z)=\bigotimes_i \sigma_i(\cdot\mid z),
\]

so its unconditional law is a public mixture of product laws. For private recommendations `R=(R_i)_i`, player `i` observes only `R_i`; an obedience inequality has the form

\[
\mathbb E[u_i(R_i,R_{-i})\mid R_i=r_i]
\ge
\mathbb E[u_i(a_i',R_{-i})\mid R_i=r_i]
\]

for each recommendation `r_i` and deviation `a_i'`. Publicly revealing `R` replaces this conditional-information problem with a different one.

The landed convexification theorem says, for the relevant finite observation map,

\[
\operatorname{Image}(\text{correlated laws})
=
\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\operatorname{Image}(\text{product laws})\bigr).
\]

It does **not** say that each correlated law, or each correlated payoff point, equals the image of one product law. Exact replacement by a product law is available only after proving the desired point already lies in the product image.

## Fixed-target witness

The separator uses the private-recommendation distribution uniform on three action profiles `CC`, `CD`, and `DC`. Its payoff is exactly

\[
(5/7,5/7).
\]

No independent mixed profile reaches that target within the formal positive gap

\[
\delta_\star=\frac{41-7\sqrt{34}}{21}>0.
\]

This proves that the private target cannot simply be “deconvexified” to one ordinary root product law. The one-decision lift also has no pre-action communication phase, but the witness does not prove that every public dynamic protocol in other games fails, and it does not exclude other ordinary equilibrium targets in the same game.

## Falsifiers and missing theorem

PT-PR-1 would be falsified only by a semantics theorem showing that, under explicitly stated hypotheses, public disclosure preserves exactly the private-message obedience inequalities. No such general theorem is currently present.

A genuine positive resolution would provide an operational compiler specifying:

1. what each player observes and when;
2. how private contingent messages are generated or why they are unnecessary;
3. how unilateral deviations affect the sampling law;
4. why disclosure does not destroy obedience; and
5. how the compiled phase splices back into the stochastic game.

A genuine negative resolution would quantify over the relevant class of dynamic protocols or over all sustainable selector targets. The current production does not establish an arbitrary-behavior payoff gap, much less a semialgebraic description of such a gap.

## Exit condition

Keep the distinction as a permanent fence. Close the open part only after an exact public-to-private conversion theorem with information sets and deviation semantics lands, or after a protocol-class impossibility theorem is proved. Do not close it from convex-hull geometry alone.
