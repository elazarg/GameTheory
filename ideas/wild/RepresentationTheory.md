# Wild ideas: uniform equilibrium and representation theory

## Status

Representation theory is largely unmined in the stochastic-game development.
The repository contains neighboring structures—static symmetric games,
harmonic/mean-ergodic decompositions, finite-group public lotteries,
owner-labeled holonomy, and an owner-obstruction cokernel—but no group action on
analytic Bellman germs, no isotypic endpoint decomposition, and no module
interpretation of the processed harmonic hierarchy.

Ordinary external symmetry is useful for special games.  The more universal
candidate is the representation of the associative algebra generated by the
game's supported transition, payoff, and observation operators.

## 1. Finite-group symmetry

Let a finite group (G) act on states, players, and actions, preserving the
transition and payoff functions.  The Bellman operator is then equivariant:

\[
T_\lambda(gv)=gT_\lambda(v).
\]

The state/payoff space decomposes into isotypic components

\[
\mathbb R^S\otimes\mathbb R^I
=\bigoplus_{\rho\in\widehat G}V_\rho.
\]

Every equivariant transition kernel, resolvent, harmonic projection, and
linearized Bellman operator preserves this decomposition.  The trivial
component carries invariant/common information; nontrivial components carry
symmetry breaking, redistribution, and periodic modes.

This can reduce harmonic and Farkas calculations blockwise.  It cannot justify
averaging Nash equilibrium arcs: the equilibrium set is not convex.  One needs
an invariant branch or an equivariant degree/index theorem.

## 2. Player and distinguished-deviator representations

For an anonymous (n)-player game,

\[
\mathbb R^I=\mathbb R\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf1^\perp.
\]

The first component is common welfare; the second is redistribution among
players.  Equilibrium verification distinguishes one deviator (i), reducing
the symmetry group from (S_n) to the stabilizer (S_{n-1}).  Under that
subgroup, player space splits into:

1. the deviator coordinate;
2. the opponents' average;
3. differences among opponents.

A possible theorem for anonymous stochastic games is that unilateral Bellman
inequalities factor through the first two scalar channels, with the opponent
standard component either decoupled or separately controlled.  This would be a
real multiplayer reduction, not merely a change of basis.

Experiment E19 checks the exact projections and Reynolds averaging.

## 3. Equivariant convex duality

If a convex feasible set and the point being separated are (G)-invariant, a
dual separator may be averaged over (G).  The result is still separating and
lies in the trivial representation.  Thus invariant occupation/target
questions require only invariant dual certificates.

More generally, the owner-obstruction cokernel should inherit a (G)-module
structure:

\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_\rho\mathcal H_\rho.
\]

This could distinguish systemic obstruction classes from pure transfers,
symmetry-breaking classes, and owner-compatible punishment modes.  A nonzero
nontrivial component is not automatically harmless because deviations are
coordinatewise.

Experiment E21 formalizes the safe linear part: Reynolds averages are invariant
and commute with equivariant rational-linear maps.  It deliberately makes no
claim about averaging equilibrium strategies.

## 4. Equivariant arc selection

The analytic equilibrium variety of a symmetric game carries a group action.
Its branches have isotropy types: fully symmetric, partially symmetric, or
symmetry-breaking families in one group orbit.

Equivariant degree or equilibrium index might force a branch with a favorable
isotropy type.  This is a structured version of arc improvement:

> Select an analytic equilibrium germ whose isotropy forces a closable endpoint
> support or eliminates specified obstruction representations.

E05 already proves in a small exact family that endpoint support is
arc-dependent.  What is missing is an index theorem connecting symmetry type to
an atlas leaf.

## 5. Transition-algebra representations

For a general game define the finite-dimensional algebra

\[
\mathcal A=
\operatorname{alg}
\bigl(I,\{P_a\},\{D_{g_i(a)}\},\{O_r\}\bigr)
\subseteq\operatorname{End}(\mathbb R^S),
\]

where the generators are the relevant transition matrices, payoff
multiplication operators, and public-observation projections.

Then (mathbb R^S) is an (mathcal A)-module.  This language is available even
when the game has no external automorphisms:

- common invariant subspaces are stable state features;
- quotient modules are exact state abstractions;
- the commutant (mathcal A') records hidden symmetry;
- simultaneous block triangularization separates dynamical modes;
- the Jacobson radical is a candidate home for transient/nilpotent effects;
- the semisimple quotient carries persistent modes.

Generic generators may produce the full matrix algebra, leaving no reduction.
The endpoint supported kernels can nevertheless generate a much smaller
algebra than the full game.

Experiment E18 computes exact algebra and commutant dimensions for cyclic,
nilpotent-transient, and full-matrix examples.

The Q118 predecessor atlas supplies a concrete nonlinear successor to this
picture. Its chart branches generate a semigroup of maps on continuation-value
boxes, and a cyclic Schur reduction compares the linearization of a branch
word with the full Bellman Jacobian. This is presently a local, table-specific
instance—not a controlled transition-algebra theorem. The promotion trigger
is an exact common or directed family of compatible boxes with a game-facing
branch decoder and a common pathwise contraction bound. A domain escape,
mixed-word inactive-inequality failure, or switching instability falsifies the
semigroup consumer even if every isolated branch is locally stable.

## 6. A possible canonical rank invariant

The analytic hierarchy repeatedly enlarges processed harmonic spans and passes
to quotients.  This resembles a module filtration

\[
0\subset V_0\subset V_1\subset\cdots\subset V.
\]

The decisive question is:

> Is the processed harmonic-jet span a submodule for the endpoint transition
> algebra, and is the induced filtration preserved under stopped-history
> rebasing?

If so, the associated graded dimensions, module length, or a Loewy-type rank
could provide a canonical progress invariant.  Non-recreation of discharged
modes might then follow from functoriality rather than branch-specific account
arguments.

This is the representation-theoretic direction most directly connected to the
general conjecture.  It requires an audit against the actual hierarchy types;
no current experiment establishes it.

## 7. Valued and filtered representations of analytic germs

The transition and response operators depend analytically on (lambda).  Over
an analytic or Puiseux germ field, orders of vanishing define a filtration.
Passing to leading terms produces an associated graded representation:

\[
V_{\mathrm{germ}}\longmapsto
\operatorname{gr}V=\bigoplus_qV^{(q)}.
\]

The endpoint is degree zero; higher response orders occupy later pieces;
analytic deflation exposes and deletes a leading graded face.  The current
hierarchy may therefore be a coordinate-level implementation of an associated
graded module.

A successful formulation could unify support stabilization, leading-power
selection, harmonic jets, and deflation rank.  It must preserve positivity and
owner custody, which ordinary semisimple representation theory ignores.

Moving-kernel resolvent calculations now give this direction a concrete test:
the associated graded object must recover the entire convergent Puiseux
hierarchy of prescribed and unilateral residuals, not just an ordinary
first-order bias. Exact full-germ block elimination, reward/test-row transport,
and affine rebasing are now Lean-formalized through `e0d7462`. Leading-only
reduction and every fixed coefficient count fail in general. The live module
test is therefore stronger: does the reduction close under policy choice and
owner-labelled unilateral deviations, and can any quotient compress exact
germ tails without losing strategic signs?

## 8. Fourier modes and cyclic games

For a cyclic state space, convolution kernels diagonalize in characters.  Each
mode has scalar Abel multiplier

\[
\frac{\lambda}{1-(1-\lambda)\widehat P_\lambda(\chi)}
\]

and Cesaro multiplier

\[
\frac1N\sum_{t<N}\widehat P_\lambda(\chi)^t.
\]

For a slow kernel

\[
P_\lambda=(1-c\lambda)I+c\lambda C,
\]

every nontrivial Fourier mode retains a nonzero Abel boundary-layer multiplier,
while its fixed-policy Cesaro multiplier vanishes.  Retargeting can therefore
be read character by character.

Experiment E20 demonstrates this numerically and checks the closed-form mode
limit.

## 9. Cohomology and owner custody

Potential equations are cohomological equations.  Graph edge charges are
coboundaries exactly when cycle holonomy vanishes; Markov rewards satisfy a
Poisson equation (f=h-Ph) when their invariant-measure obstruction vanishes.

Owner labels make the coefficient space a player representation.  The existing
owner holonomy and cokernel can therefore be viewed as a representation-valued
first cohomology.  The useful next result is not terminology but an exact
decomposition of obstruction classes into strategic representation channels.

Experiments E03 and E06 provide the finite coboundary and monodromy models.

## Highest-value standalone questions

1. **Module invariance audit.** Is every processed harmonic-jet span invariant
   under the endpoint supported transition algebra?
2. **Controlled-policy functoriality.** Does exact block reduction induce one
   finite controlled quotient whose legal policies, owner-labelled deviations,
   and stopped-history rebases correspond to the original system?
3. **Positive irreducible modes.** Which irreducible real modules of an algebra
   generated by stochastic matrices can carry positive occupation vectors?
4. **Owner-isotypic obstruction.** Does the owner cokernel decompose naturally
   under game automorphisms, and which components are strategically inert?
5. **Equivariant germ selection.** Under what conditions does a symmetric
   stochastic game admit an analytic stationary-equilibrium germ of prescribed
   isotropy or positive index?
6. **Fourier benchmark.** Which representation modes carry the obstruction in
   cyclic multiplayer examples such as FTV-type games?
7. **Associated-graded closure.** Can every nonterminal graded endpoint mode be
   converted into a credible response, a lower module layer, or an explicitly
   discharged account?

## Priority assessment

External group symmetry is a useful special-case accelerator.  Transition
algebras and valued/filtered modules are the potentially general contribution.
The predecessor-branch semigroup sharpens the transition-algebra test but does
not change this priority assessment or prove controlled Schur closure in
general.
The classical exact confluence/rebase audit is closed. The priority test is now
controlled-policy closure and compression: recover the full Puiseux residual
filtration while quotienting choices, not merely one prescribed affine system.
Failure would delimit the analogy sharply; success could supply the missing
canonical controlled progress language.
